# Public-data findings

## Analysis scope

This analysis reviews monthly complaint volumes reported in the AFCA Datacube for the 2025–26 financial year. It is intended to identify demand patterns that may inform the complaint-management case study.

The figures are aggregated monthly totals. They do not contain case-level details, processing times, employee workloads or customer outcomes.

## Key observations

### 1. Complaint demand remained consistently high

AFCA received 117,028 complaints during the reporting period, averaging approximately 9,752 complaints per month.

Monthly volumes remained above 8,000 throughout the year, suggesting that complaint intake represents a continuous operational workload rather than an occasional spike.

### 2. Demand varied meaningfully between months

The lowest volume occurred in December 2025, with 8,267 complaints received. The highest occurred in May 2026, with 10,759 complaints.

This represents a difference of 2,492 complaints between the lowest and highest months. The largest monthly decline occurred in November 2025, when complaints fell by 13.9% compared with October.

### 3. Demand increased during the later part of the year

Complaint volumes exceeded 10,000 in every month from March to June 2026. March recorded an increase of 1,062 complaints compared with February.

This sustained increase may create additional pressure on case intake, initial assessment and allocation if operational capacity does not increase at the same pace.

### 4. Process activity also changed across the year

Complaints resolved during Registration and Referral, progressed to Case Management, and closed at Case Management generally declined toward December–February before increasing again later in the year.

These measures should not be treated as a single conversion funnel. Complaints closed during a particular month may have been received before the reporting period, while the published figures do not identify whether the same complaints appear across each measure.

## Implications for the case study

The public data supports investigating how a complaint-management solution could provide:

- clearer visibility of complaint volumes and case stages;
- consistent triage and prioritisation rules;
- fair and transparent case allocation;
- monitoring of workload and emerging demand changes;
- alerts for cases requiring attention;
- controlled access to sensitive complaint information; and
- consistent management reporting.

These are proposed areas for further analysis rather than confirmed problems within AFCA. They would need to be validated through stakeholder interviews, internal process information and case-level operational data.

## Additional data required

A more detailed operational assessment would require information such as:

- complaint received and status-change dates;
- complaint type, product and complexity;
- priority and vulnerability indicators;
- current case status and assigned team;
- allocation and reassignment history;
- response and resolution timeframes;
- overdue or ageing cases; and
- access roles and audit history.

## Limitations

The dataset was manually transcribed from the AFCA Datacube and checked for record count, missing values and totals. It contains aggregated public information only.

The analysis cannot determine case backlog, employee productivity, service-level performance, complaint causes or true resolution rates. No such conclusions should be drawn without supporting case-level data.
