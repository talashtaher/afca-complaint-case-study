# Analysis approach

## Purpose

This analysis uses AFCA’s public monthly complaint volumes to identify demand patterns that could inform case visibility, workload monitoring and capacity planning.

It is not intended to assess AFCA’s actual operational performance because the public dataset does not include backlog, staffing, case age, service levels or individual complaint journeys.

## Questions

1. What was the typical monthly volume of complaints received?
2. Which months recorded the highest and lowest incoming demand?
3. How much did incoming demand change from month to month?
4. How did activity across Registration and Referral and Case Management vary during the year?
5. What additional operational information would managers need to support triage, allocation and performance monitoring?

## Measures

The analysis will use:

- total and average monthly complaints received
- highest and lowest monthly complaint volumes
- month-on-month change in complaints received
- three-month rolling average of complaints received
- monthly trends in R&R resolutions, progressions and closures

## Interpretation boundaries

The monthly measures represent activity at different process stages and may contain complaints from different intake periods.

Therefore, the analysis will not calculate or claim:

- a true resolution rate
- a true progression rate
- a true closure rate
- backlog size
- staff productivity
- service-level compliance
- reasons for monthly changes

Where the public data suggests a possible operational concern, it will be recorded as a hypothesis requiring further evidence rather than a confirmed finding.
