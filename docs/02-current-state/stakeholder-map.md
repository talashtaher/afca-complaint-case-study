# Preliminary stakeholder map

## Purpose

This stakeholder map identifies the groups likely to participate in, support or be affected by the complaint-management process.

It is based on AFCA’s publicly described complaint stages and the needs of the proposed case study. It is not presented as a confirmed AFCA organisational structure.

## Stakeholder groups

| Stakeholder group | Interest in the process | Likely information or system needs | Evidence status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Complainants | Submit complaints, provide supporting information and receive updates or outcomes | Accessible submission, clear status information, secure communication and fair treatment | Publicly identified process participant |
| Financial firms or superannuation funds | Respond to referred complaints and provide information required during resolution | Clear requests, response deadlines, secure document exchange and visibility of required actions | Publicly identified process participant |
| Registration and Referral function | Receives complaints, refers them to the relevant financial firm and supports the initial stage | Complete intake information, duplicate checks, initial classification, referral tracking and alerts | Function inferred from AFCA’s published process stage |
| Case Management function | Manages complaints accepted into Case Management | Case history, documents, priority, ownership, activities, deadlines and decision records | Function inferred from AFCA’s published process stage |
| Resolution or decision-making roles | Support agreement, assessment or formal complaint outcomes | Complete evidence, communication history, reasoning records and controlled approvals | Role group inferred from published outcome descriptions |
| Operational leadership | Oversees demand, workload, allocation and service performance | Workload dashboards, ageing information, exceptions, capacity indicators and escalation visibility | Case-study assumption requiring validation |
| Data and reporting users | Prepare operational and management reporting | Consistent definitions, reliable data, reporting access and data-quality controls | Case-study assumption requiring validation |
| Technology and system support | Maintains system availability, configuration and user access | Support tools, configuration controls, monitoring and change records | Case-study assumption requiring validation |
| Privacy, security and governance roles | Protect sensitive information and oversee compliance obligations | Role-based access, audit history, retention controls and security monitoring | Case-study assumption requiring validation |

## Working engagement view

### Manage closely

- Registration and Referral function
- Case Management function
- operational leadership
- privacy, security and governance roles

These groups would have significant involvement in process design, prioritisation, allocation, access and operational controls.

### Consult regularly

- complainants;
- financial firms or superannuation funds;
- resolution or decision-making roles;
- data and reporting users; and
- technology and system support.

Their input would be required to understand communication needs, evidence handling, reporting requirements and system constraints.

## Initial stakeholder needs

The preliminary stakeholder analysis suggests that the proposed solution should consider:

- clear complaint ownership and status visibility;
- consistent triage and prioritisation;
- fair and traceable allocation;
- secure handling of documents and personal information;
- reminders and escalation for time-sensitive actions;
- a complete history of decisions and communications; and
- reliable operational and management reporting.

These needs are starting hypotheses. They should not be treated as confirmed requirements until they have been validated with the relevant stakeholders.

## Validation questions

1. Which roles receive, register and initially assess a complaint?
2. Who can assign, reassign or change the priority of a case?
3. What information is required before a complaint can progress?
4. How are urgent, vulnerable or complex complaints identified?
5. Which users may view or update sensitive information?
6. What deadlines, alerts and escalation rules are currently used?
7. How do financial firms submit responses and supporting documents?
8. What workload and performance information do managers require?
9. Which actions must be retained in the audit history?
10. Who approves process, system or reporting changes?

## Limitations

This is a preliminary stakeholder map developed from public information and case-study assumptions. Stakeholder titles, responsibilities, influence and system needs would require confirmation through interviews, workshops and internal documentation.
