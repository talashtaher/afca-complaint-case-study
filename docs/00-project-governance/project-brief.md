# Project Brief

## Project title

Improving Financial Complaint Triage and Case Management: An Independent AFCA Public-Data Case Study

## Author

Talash Bin Taher

## Context

AFCA independently assists consumers and small businesses to make and resolve complaints about financial firms. Its public Datacube provides comparative complaint information, while AFCA also publishes a high-level description of its complaint-resolution process.

The volume and variety of financial complaints make this a useful public case for examining service operations, triage, case allocation, customer visibility and performance reporting. This project will use public evidence to develop an independent improvement proposal.

## Business question

How could complaint triage, allocation, case visibility and performance monitoring be improved while maintaining fairness, privacy, accessibility and procedural consistency during periods of high complaint demand?

## Objectives

1. Analyse published complaint demand and resolution activity.
2. Document the publicly described complaint journey and key stakeholders.
3. Identify evidence-supported improvement opportunities and validation gaps.
4. Design a future-state process for triage, allocation and case visibility.
5. Define prioritised and testable business, functional, data and non-functional requirements.
6. Specify management information that would support timely operational decisions.
7. Demonstrate requirements traceability, UAT thinking and implementation planning.

## In scope

- Publicly documented complaint intake and case progression
- Initial information capture and completeness checks
- Triage and priority assessment
- Case routing and allocation concepts
- Case status and communication visibility
- Operational measures and dashboard requirements
- Business, functional, data and non-functional requirements
- User stories, acceptance criteria, traceability and UAT
- Risks, change impacts and phased implementation recommendations

## Out of scope

- Claims about AFCA's internal technology or employee practices
- Legal interpretation of individual complaints
- Recommending outcomes for individual disputes
- Building or configuring a production case-management platform
- Estimating financial benefits without a defensible public basis
- Processing personal or confidential complaint information
- Representing this work as commissioned by AFCA

## Intended portfolio audience

- Entry-level Business Analyst hiring managers
- Junior Data Analyst and Reporting Analyst hiring managers
- Financial services, insurance, government and regulated-service employers
- Recruiters evaluating process, requirements, data and communication skills

## Success criteria

The case study is successful if a reviewer can:

- trace every major factual claim to a credible source;
- distinguish facts, findings, assumptions, hypotheses and recommendations;
- follow the reasoning from evidence to problem, requirement and test;
- understand the process diagrams without a verbal explanation;
- reproduce the main calculations from the included data and logic;
- see realistic prioritisation and scope control; and
- question the author in an interview and receive a clear, defensible explanation.

## Key constraints

- Only public information is available.
- Public data may be aggregated and may not expose operational causes.
- The project must avoid implying access to internal stakeholders.
- The work must be achievable in approximately ten focused days.

## Initial deliverables

1. Evidence and source register
2. Data profile and baseline analysis
3. Stakeholder map
4. Current-state process map
5. Pain-point and root-cause hypothesis log
6. Future-state process map
7. Requirements catalogue and prioritised backlog
8. KPI dictionary and Power BI dashboard
9. Data dictionary and requirements traceability matrix
10. UAT scenarios, risk register and implementation roadmap

## Ethical position

The analysis will not infer misconduct, poor employee performance or system failure from aggregate complaint volumes alone. Any claim that cannot be proven from public evidence will be recorded as an assumption or a hypothesis requiring validation.
