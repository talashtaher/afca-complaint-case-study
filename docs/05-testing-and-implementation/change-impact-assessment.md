# Change-Impact Assessment

## Purpose

This assessment identifies how the proposed complaint-management solution may affect people, processes, information and controls.

It is based on the proposed future state rather than confirmed AFCA internal arrangements. Roles, impact levels and readiness actions require stakeholder validation.

## Impact scale

| Level | Meaning |
|---|---|
| High | Material change to responsibilities, decisions or daily work |
| Medium | Noticeable change requiring guidance, communication or training |
| Low | Limited change that can generally be managed through standard communication |

## Stakeholder impacts

| Stakeholder group | Proposed change | Potential impact | Level | Readiness action |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Complaint-intake and triage users | Structured validation, duplicate warnings and explainable triage recommendations | New data-entry, review and override responsibilities | High | Scenario-based training and approved decision guidance |
| Case workers | Central case view, tasks, alerts, structured history and handovers | Greater visibility and more consistent recording expectations | High | Role-based workflow training and supported practice |
| Team leaders | Workload, ageing, exception and reassignment visibility | Increased responsibility for monitoring and intervention | High | Dashboard interpretation and escalation training |
| Allocation users | Workload-aware recommendations and recorded overrides | Allocation decisions become more transparent and auditable | High | Rule explanation and override-governance training |
| Decision or resolution roles | Structured handover, case history and closure checks | More consistent information at later stages | Medium | Handover and closure-process guidance |
| Reporting users | Approved measures and controlled definitions | Existing reports may require reconciliation or replacement | High | Data-definition workshops and report validation |
| Business administrators | Controlled maintenance of rules, categories and templates | New governance and version-control responsibilities | High | Configuration and approval-process training |
| Privacy and security functions | Role access, sensitive-data controls and audit monitoring | Additional design, approval and monitoring activity | Medium | Early control review and operational procedures |
| Technology and support functions | Monitoring, integrations, errors and support documentation | New support dependencies and diagnostic requirements | High | Support model, runbooks and escalation preparation |
| Complainants and representatives | Potentially clearer and more accessible communication | Communication experience may change | Medium | Accessibility review and communication testing |
| Financial firms or super funds | Potentially more structured referral and response information | Interaction formats or expectations may change | Medium | External communication and transition guidance |
| Operational leadership | More consistent workload and performance information | New measures require careful interpretation | Medium | Reporting definitions and decision-use guidance |

## Change by dimension

| Dimension | Proposed impact | Risk if unmanaged | Required preparation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Process | More structured intake, triage, allocation, handover and closure | Workarounds or inconsistent adoption | Approved procedures and process ownership |
| Roles | Clearer ownership, review and override responsibilities | Confusion or duplicated accountability | Role mapping and responsibility confirmation |
| Data | Additional structured fields and governed definitions | Poor-quality data or excessive collection | Data ownership, validation and privacy review |
| Technology | Central workflow, alerts, access and reporting capabilities | Support failures or low user confidence | Environment readiness, support model and training |
| Governance | Controlled rules, audit history and configuration changes | Unapproved changes or unclear accountability | Approval authorities and change control |
| Reporting | Agreed operational measures and definitions | Conflicting reports or inappropriate conclusions | Baselines, definitions and reconciliation |
| Communication | Templates, preferences and accessibility information | Inappropriate or inaccessible communication | Content review and accessibility testing |
| External interaction | Potential changes to referral, evidence and response handling | Confusion for external participants | Clear transition communications and support |

## Training needs

| Training area | Intended audience | Proposed method |
|---|---|---|
| Future-state process and responsibilities | All affected internal users | Facilitated overview and process walkthrough |
| Case creation and validation | Intake users | Demonstration and hands-on scenarios |
| Triage, priority and human review | Triage and authorised review users | Scenario-based decision practice |
| Allocation and override governance | Allocation users and team leaders | Rules workshop and practical exercises |
| Case tasks, alerts and handovers | Case workers | Role-based workflow simulation |
| Privacy, security and appropriate information handling | All authorised users | Mandatory control training |
| Reporting definitions and limitations | Leaders and reporting users | Measure walkthrough and reconciliation exercises |
| Rule and configuration administration | Authorised administrators | Controlled administrator training |
| Support and incident handling | Technology and support users | Runbook rehearsal and escalation simulation |

## Communication needs

Change communications should explain:

- why the change is being introduced;
- what is changing and what remains unchanged;
- which decisions remain the responsibility of people;
- when each implementation phase begins;
- where users can obtain guidance and support;
- how feedback and defects will be recorded;
- how sensitive information must be handled; and
- how readiness and adoption will be assessed.

## Readiness indicators

Before each implementation phase, confirm:

- affected roles and responsibilities are agreed;
- process guidance is available;
- required training has been completed;
- user access has been tested;
- support arrangements are operational;
- known limitations are communicated;
- data and reporting definitions are approved;
- privacy and accessibility reviews are complete;
- transition and fallback arrangements are understood; and
- accountable stakeholders approve progression.

## Validation questions

1. Which teams and external participants will experience the greatest change?
2. Which current activities will stop, start or continue?
3. Which role changes require formal approval?
4. What training methods are appropriate for each audience?
5. What resistance or adoption barriers are likely?
6. What support capacity is required after release?
7. How will readiness and adoption be measured?
8. Which communications require privacy, legal or accessibility review?
