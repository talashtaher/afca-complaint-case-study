# Phased Implementation Roadmap

## Purpose

This roadmap proposes a controlled sequence for validating, delivering and embedding the complaint-management solution.

It does not assign dates, costs or confirmed resources. Those decisions require stakeholder approval, delivery estimation and technical assessment.

## Phase 0 — Validate and mobilise

### Objective

Confirm the business need, scope, governance and delivery readiness.

### Proposed activities

- validate current-state risks and stakeholder needs;
- approve business requirements and solution boundaries;
- confirm roles, decision rights and requirement owners;
- baseline operational measures where reliable data exists;
- complete privacy, security, accessibility and records reviews;
- assess technology, integration and migration options;
- confirm delivery approach, resources and governance; and
- approve the initial backlog and acceptance approach.

### Exit conditions

- scope and requirements are approved;
- major assumptions have been validated or assigned;
- delivery governance and ownership are confirmed;
- critical control requirements are understood; and
- unresolved risks are accepted or have treatment plans.

## Phase 1 — Establish the controlled case foundation

### Objective

Create a reliable, secure and traceable case record.

### Proposed capabilities

- role-based access;
- unique case creation;
- mandatory-field validation;
- case stage, status, priority and ownership;
- chronological case history;
- basic tasks and due dates; and
- audit records.

### Exit conditions

- core case workflows pass agreed testing;
- access and audit controls are accepted;
- required data is captured consistently;
- support procedures are available; and
- authorised users are ready for controlled use.

## Phase 2 — Introduce triage and allocation support

### Objective

Support consistent triage and workload-aware allocation while retaining human accountability.

### Proposed capabilities

- duplicate and related-case warnings;
- classification and triage recommendations;
- recommendation explanations;
- priority and vulnerability review;
- allocation recommendations;
- controlled overrides; and
- reassignment history.

### Exit conditions

- rules and reference data are approved;
- users can explain recommendations;
- override reasons are recorded;
- fairness and privacy reviews are complete; and
- representative scenarios pass UAT.

## Phase 3 — Strengthen case workflow and communication

### Objective

Improve visibility and continuity across active case work.

### Proposed capabilities

- alerts and exception handling;
- structured handovers;
- communication and document records;
- communication preferences and accessibility needs;
- approved templates;
- outcome and closure validation; and
- controlled case reopening.

### Exit conditions

- handovers contain required information;
- alerts and exceptions have accountable owners;
- communication and document access is controlled;
- accessibility scenarios are accepted; and
- closure and reopening history is preserved.

## Phase 4 — Enable oversight and reporting

### Objective

Provide reliable operational visibility and management information.

### Proposed capabilities

- workload and ageing views;
- priority, stage and exception monitoring;
- approved management measures;
- report definitions and limitations;
- reconciled reporting outputs; and
- adoption and data-quality monitoring.

### Exit conditions

- measures are approved and reproducible;
- reports reconcile to agreed sources;
- access to operational information is appropriate;
- users understand reporting limitations; and
- success measures have approved baselines and targets.

## Phase 5 — Optimise and transition to continuous improvement

### Objective

Improve the solution using controlled operational evidence.

### Proposed activities

- review adoption, overrides, exceptions and alert effectiveness;
- assess allocation and reassignment patterns;
- refine approved rules and templates;
- address recurring data-quality issues;
- complete post-implementation review;
- prioritise deferred requirements;
- transition ownership into business-as-usual governance; and
- maintain an improvement backlog.

### Exit conditions

- operational ownership is confirmed;
- residual risks are accepted or treated;
- support demand has stabilised;
- improvement decisions use reliable evidence; and
- benefits and lessons learned are documented.

## Cross-phase controls

The following controls apply throughout implementation:

- human accountability for complaint decisions;
- privacy and data minimisation;
- role-based access;
- accessible design and testing;
- requirement and decision traceability;
- risk and issue management;
- controlled configuration changes;
- test evidence and defect management;
- stakeholder communication; and
- release and rollback decisions.

## Key dependencies

| Dependency | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Approved process and requirement ownership | Enables decisions and prevents unresolved ambiguity |
| Confirmed business rules | Required for triage, priority, allocation and alerts |
| Data definitions and quality | Required for migration, workflows and reporting |
| Role and access model | Required for privacy, security and accountability |
| Technology and integration assessment | Determines feasibility and implementation effort |
| Accessibility review | Required before approving representative workflows |
| Test environments and data | Required for reliable UAT and regression testing |
| Training and support readiness | Required for controlled adoption |
| Reporting baselines | Required to evaluate benefits and operational effects |

## Roadmap governance

A phase should not progress solely because planned activities have been completed. The authorised governance group should consider:

- exit-condition evidence;
- unresolved defects and risks;
- user and operational readiness;
- privacy, security and accessibility findings;
- data reconciliation results;
- support and fallback readiness; and
- expected value compared with implementation risk.
